## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is Shopify Magic's AI product description generator actually worth using for an online store?

It's worth using, but only as a drafting tool, not as a replacement for thinking about your product and customer. I've worked with DTC brands since Shopify Magic launched with the Summer '23 Edition, and the results split pretty cleanly into two groups.

The first group already knew their positioning. They knew who the buyer was, what objections came up, and what made the product different. For them, Magic saved real time. They generated a first draft, then rewrote it with specific details, customer language, and proof such as reviews, ingredients, materials, or results. Their pages improved and their teams moved faster.

The second group hoped the tool would supply the strategy. They bulk-generated descriptions across the catalog, published them lightly edited, and ended up with pages that sounded polished but interchangeable. Conversion rates barely changed, because the writing was never the bottleneck. The bottleneck was unclear differentiation and weak trust signals.

Here is how I'd use it today:

1. Write down your customer's top three hesitations before you prompt anything.
2. Feed the tool real product facts, not just a product name.
3. Treat the output as a rough draft and cut every generic claim.
4. Test a handful of key pages against your existing copy instead of replacing everything at once.
5. Judge success by conversion and revenue per visitor, not by how many descriptions you produced.

If you are launching hundreds of SKUs, the time savings are real. If you have ten hero products, hand-crafted copy will usually win.
