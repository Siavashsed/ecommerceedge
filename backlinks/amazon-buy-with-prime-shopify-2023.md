## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should a small DTC Shopify brand add Amazon's Buy with Prime button to its store?

For most DTC brands doing under about $5M a year, my answer is to wait. That isn't because the button fails. It works, and that's exactly why it's easy to misjudge.

I'm an ecommerce consultant and I work mostly with DTC brands that are trying to scale. Earlier this year, before Buy with Prime became a native Shopify app, I installed it on a client's supplement product page using the older script setup. The conversion lift showed up fast. By week three, Prime orders were 22% of revenue on that page. Anyone looking only at conversion rate would have called it a win.

The problem is what a conversion dashboard leaves out. Here is what I'd look at before adding it:

1. Margin per order. Buy with Prime runs on Amazon fulfillment, which comes with fees. Work out contribution margin on Prime orders compared with your normal orders, not just revenue.

2. Who owns the customer. A shopper who checks out with their Amazon account trusts Amazon, not your brand. That matters for email capture, subscriptions and your next launch.

3. Repeat purchase behavior. This is the big one. If Prime buyers reorder through Amazon instead of your site, you paid to acquire a customer for someone else. You won't see it at week three. You need at least 60 days of data.

4. Inventory complexity. Splitting stock between your own fulfillment and Amazon's network adds forecasting risk that small teams often underestimate.

If your retention is strong, your margins are healthy and you can measure all four, testing it makes sense. Without that data you're buying a short-term lift that may cost you long-term value.
