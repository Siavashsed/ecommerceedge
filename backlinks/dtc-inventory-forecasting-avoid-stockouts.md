## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should a fast-growing DTC brand forecast inventory to avoid stockouts?

The short answer: forecast from your growth rate, not from your past sales.

Most DTC brands build reorder plans on trailing averages, like last month's sales or a 90-day run rate. That works for a flat business. For a growing one it fails without anyone noticing, because any average of past demand trails behind current demand. The faster you grow, the bigger that gap gets, and it is biggest in your best months.

I saw this clearly in March 2024 with a skincare client. They had 2,400 units of their hero serum and a reorder sheet saying they had six weeks of cover. They sold out in 31 hours. Nobody made a math error. The sheet answered "what did we sell?" when the real question was "what will we sell at the rate we are growing now?"

Here is what I recommend:

1. Project demand forward. Take your recent month-over-month growth rate and apply it to the coming weeks, instead of assuming next month looks like last month.

2. Tie safety stock to lead time. A buffer stated as a flat number of weeks means nothing if your supplier needs 10 weeks and demand is speeding up. Size it to lead time plus how much demand swings.

3. Plan for spikes. Launches, promos, influencer posts and press can multiply demand overnight. Build those into the forecast before they happen.

4. Review weekly while you scale. A monthly check is too slow when demand compounds.

5. Watch weeks of cover, not units on hand. Units look comforting. Weeks of cover at your projected rate tells you the truth.
