## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do ecommerce site search results fail and how can I fix zero-result searches?

Zero-result searches usually come from one root cause: your customers and your catalog use different words. Fixing that is the fastest win in ecommerce site search, and it's more plumbing than strategy.

I once reviewed a skincare store where the top failed query was "vitamin c serum," searched 1,900 times a month with zero results. The store sold exactly that product, but it was titled "Brightening Ascorbic Complex." The search engine could not connect the two, so some of the store's most motivated shoppers hit a dead end and left.

Here's how I'd approach it:

1. Export your failed queries. Pull 90 days of zero-result searches from your search analytics (Shopify, Google Analytics site search, or your search app) and sort by volume.

2. Sort failures into buckets. Typically they're synonyms ("moisturiser" vs "cream"), spelling variants and typos, ingredient or benefit terms missing from titles, and products you genuinely don't carry.

3. Fix the first three buckets with synonym rules, alias or tag fields, and typo tolerance. Most search tools support these natively.

4. Redirect the rest. For queries about things you don't stock, send people to the closest collection or show a useful fallback with bestsellers instead of an empty page.

5. Review monthly. Language drifts as trends change, so this is a recurring habit, not a one-time project.

The reason this matters is intent. People who use the search box have usually decided what they want, so a failed search costs you a near-certain sale rather than a casual browser.
