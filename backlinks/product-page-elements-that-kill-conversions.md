## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why is my ecommerce product page getting traffic but not converting?

I'm an ecommerce consultant who works with DTC brands, and when traffic is healthy but conversion is low, the cause is almost never one big flaw. It's usually several small points of friction that make the shopper work. Shoppers won't work hard for a $38 product.

Here is where I look first, in this order.

1. Load speed on a real phone connection. Test on cellular, not wifi. In one audit I ran, a hero video took 9 seconds to show a single frame of the product. Mobile conversion was 1.1%, and the founder had just spent $6,000 on a redesign that never addressed it. Compress or replace heavy hero media, serve properly sized images, and make sure the product appears almost instantly.

2. Surprise costs. A shipping fee that only appears at checkout is one of the most common reasons people abandon. Show shipping cost, or a free-shipping threshold, on the product page itself.

3. Buried trust signals. Reviews, return policy and delivery times should be visible without scrolling far. If a shopper has to hunt for reassurance, many won't.

4. Mobile usability. Check that variant selectors, quantity fields and the add-to-cart button are easy to use with one thumb, and that the button stays visible.

5. Unclear value. Within the first screen, a visitor should understand what it is, who it's for, what it costs and when it arrives.

A simple way to start: pull up your page on your own phone, on cellular, and time how long until you could actually buy. Then list every moment you hesitated. That list is your fix queue, and it's cheaper than a redesign.
